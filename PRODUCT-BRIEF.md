# New Radical product brief

## The concept

New Radical is a short daily challenge that helps young athletes see their game earlier. Each day, an athlete receives one pressure moment from the sport and position they selected. The play unfolds visually, the athlete makes a decision before the reveal, and a coach explains the strongest read.

It combines Wordle's daily ritual, Duolingo's habit and shared commitment, Strava's real-world athletic identity, and the durable squad belonging of team-based games. It should not feel like schoolwork. It should feel like proving that you understand the game.

## Audience

The initial audience is competitive youth athletes and their families, with room to expand to high-school athletes, clubs, teams, coaches, and adult recreational players. The tone should respect athletes rather than speak down to children.

Supported launch sports:

- Baseball
- Basketball
- Soccer
- Football
- Volleyball
- Jiu-jitsu

## Core promise

**Think you see the game? Prove it.**

One moment. One call. Every day. Watch a pressure play unfold, lock your decision, and then see what the coach saw. Build Game IQ, beat friends, contribute to a squad, and finish the season.

## Why the daily format matters

The product should deliberately limit the primary challenge to one meaningful play per day. A shared daily moment creates anticipation, comparison, discussion, and a clean reason to return. It avoids the disposable behavior of an endless content feed.

The daily challenge should take approximately three to five minutes. Optional Clinic lessons may extend the session, but the daily win must remain achievable.

## Daily challenge flow

1. The athlete opens today's challenge.
2. A scoreboard establishes pressure and context.
3. A short top-down or cinematic simulation animates the developing play.
4. The athlete chooses A, B, or C.
5. The answer locks before the coach reveal.
6. The product clearly states the correct letter and answer.
7. A concise coach explanation teaches what signal mattered.
8. The athlete sees the response split and optionally their squad's choices.
9. The result updates points, streak, Decision DNA, squad contribution, and season progress.

## Sport personalization

Selecting a sport must transform the experience, not merely change a label. It should change:

- hero athlete;
- position language;
- play situation;
- field or court simulation;
- answer choices;
- coach's read;
- skill attributes emphasized;
- Clinic lessons;
- comparison groups.

Initial hero representation:

- Baseball: male athlete
- Basketball: male athlete
- Soccer: female athlete
- Football: male athlete
- Volleyball: female athlete
- Jiu-jitsu: male athlete

## Decision DNA

Every answer contributes to a persistent athlete profile. The initial attributes are:

- Aggression
- Discipline
- Vision
- Patience

These are not simple right/wrong grades. They should reveal tendencies, improvement, and how the athlete handles pressure. A player may eventually earn identity descriptions such as Unshaken or Uncoachable based on their pattern of decisions.

The profile should show:

- attribute scores and recent movement;
- calls completed;
- accuracy against the coach's read;
- progress by sport and position;
- streak and points;
- earned Marks;
- season history;
- an archive of previous calls.

## Arena and squads

A friends list alone is not enough. Athletes form squads with a name, crest, colors, and shared weekly identity. Different sports contribute equally to one squad.

Arena comparisons should include:

- Squad
- Division or peer group
- Everyone

Weekly boards reset so a new player can compete, while permanent accomplishments remain on the profile. Participation should be capped or normalized so one obsessive player cannot carry the entire squad. Bonuses should reward several different members completing the daily play.

## Clinic

Clinic turns mistakes into personalized short lessons. If a player repeatedly misses aggressive reads, spacing decisions, timing cues, or patience moments, the app recommends a targeted coach-led lesson.

Clinic content should be organized by sport, position, attribute, and recurring decision pattern. Lessons should be short enough to feel like an edge, not homework.

## Seasons

The initial season is **Season 01: Built Under Pressure** and runs for eight weeks.

Suggested four-act progression:

1. Awaken
2. Build
3. Pressure
4. Prove It

Permanent player history and earned Marks do not disappear. The weekly board and seasonal mission track can reset.

## Rewards and apparel

Apparel is the physical expression of the game, not unrelated merchandise.

- The main New Radical collection is available to everyone.
- Completing a season may unlock access to an exclusive finisher shirt.
- Completing a play threshold may include a physical woven Season Mark with a qualifying item.
- Winning a board may unlock a squad name or number treatment.
- Digital season crests and Marks remain permanently on the athlete profile.

Avoid unexplained fashion terms such as “colorway” in consumer-facing copy. Say “exclusive shirt,” “finisher shirt,” or describe the specific reward directly.

## Habit and social systems

Use:

- daily streaks;
- one shared challenge;
- response splits;
- friend and squad comparisons;
- weekly boards;
- squad participation goals;
- permanent earned Marks;
- season progression;
- shareable, spoiler-safe result cards.

Avoid:

- loot boxes;
- random rewards;
- punishing total streak loss;
- many currencies;
- endless feeds;
- excessive red notification dots;
- pay-to-win progression.

## MVP data and administration

The system needs structured records for users, athlete profiles, sports, positions, challenges, answer options, correct answers, coach explanations, simulations, responses, streaks, attributes, squads, squad membership, weekly standings, seasons, missions, rewards, Clinic lessons, and unlocks.

An administrator should be able to create and schedule daily challenges without editing production code. Each challenge should support sport, position, scenario copy, simulation configuration, answers, correct answer, explanation, response split, skill tags, and publish date.

## Success criteria for the MVP

The MVP succeeds if a new athlete can:

1. Understand the idea immediately.
2. Choose a sport and create a player profile.
3. Complete a convincing daily play simulation.
4. Receive a clear coach reveal.
5. See meaningful personal progress.
6. Invite friends into a squad.
7. Understand what the season and rewards offer.
8. Feel a reason to return tomorrow.
